EO Investigation · Mato Grosso, Brazil · Season 2024–25 EUDR Non-Compliant

Drought and deforestation converge over Brazil's largest maize belt

Sentinel-2 imagery processed via the Google Earth Engine Python API reveals a compressed NDVI curve across 9.8 million hectares of Mato Grosso. Combined with an 8.3% post-2020 land conversion rate, the highest of all 20 regions in this monitoring system, the region scores as non-compliant under the EU Deforestation Regulation.

§1 Explain the problem

What is happening, and why it matters

The region entered the monitoring pipeline on 15 January 2025, when soybean residue from the preceding safra crop was still visible from orbit across the eastern cerrado plateau. The first Sentinel-2 composite, computed on COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED over the bounding box 58.0°W–50.0°W, 16.0°S–10.0°S, returned a mean NDVI of 0.38 for January, against a five-year reference mean of 0.44 for the same geometry and calendar window.

By peak vegetative stage in March, the gap had widened. The 2024–25 curve reached a maximum of 0.52, against a reference peak of 0.68, a 0.16 deficit that persisted through grain-fill and senescence. Across 9.8 million hectares, that is roughly the growing area of South Korea under sustained stress.

The deforestation problem

Drought stress is not the most consequential signal from this region. Land conversion analysis places 8.3% of the agricultural area as having undergone land use change after 1 January 2020, the cutoff date under the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR). This is the highest figure of any region in this monitoring system, ahead of Kano State, Nigeria (4.1%) and East Java, Indonesia (2.7%).

Under EUDR Article 3, commodity crops, including maize, produced on land deforested or degraded after that date cannot be placed on the EU market without a due-diligence statement attesting to compliance. Brazil supplies approximately 30% of EU maize imports. Mato Grosso alone accounts for a substantial share of Brazil's second-crop (safrinha) maize, which is typically harvested in June and July, the period this season's most acute stress falls within.

§3 How it was measured

How it was measured

The pipeline authenticates to Google Earth Engine using a dedicated service account and queries the COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED image collection. Per-scene cloud cover is filtered to <20%. NDVI is computed on atmospherically corrected surface reflectance as (B8 − B4) / (B8 + B4) and reduced to monthly mean composites over the region bounding box using GEE's .mean().reduceRegion() pipeline.

What this cannot tell you

Caveats and limits
  • NDVI at bounding-box resolution cannot distinguish crop stress in standing fields from normal senescence in already-harvested blocks. The January 0.38 value may reflect late planting, fallow, or early-season drought; the data does not resolve that.
  • The 8.3% land conversion figure derives from spatial analysis of the region boundary. It identifies converted land within the monitoring footprint but does not establish which parcels supplied any specific shipment. For EUDR due-diligence purposes, parcel-level geolocation verification against the European Commission's risk classification is required.
  • Cloud cover in the humid tropics reduces usable observations. Months with fewer than three cloud-free scenes are less reliable; this monitoring system does not flag per-composite scene count.
  • Yield forecasts are computed from a linear NDVI–yield model (slope, intercept) fitted at the regional scale. They indicate relative performance, not absolute production figures, and should not be used for commodity trading decisions.
